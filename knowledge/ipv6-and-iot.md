---
title: IPv6 is gradually replacing IPv4 and is what makes IoT addressing possible at scale
tags: [lecture, networking, iot]
source: Lecture 5
---

IPv4 has survived thanks to NAT (network address translation) and because replacing working hardware costs money. Even so, internet traffic is migrating, with the majority now moving to IPv6.

The lecturer linked this to the Internet of Things: huge numbers of sensing and control devices need addresses, and communication, sensing and control are the three things IoT does.

For SmartCity Accra: thousands of air, bin and drain sensors are exactly the many-devices case; in practice many devices sit behind NAT or a gateway and talk to a broker rather than being directly addressed.
