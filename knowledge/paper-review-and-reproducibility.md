Lecture 9 was student reviews of research papers. Rules from the instructor:
- Each presenter writes a review report that is shorter than the paper and constructive: strengths, weaknesses, and a proposed improvement.
- Check whether a paper really matches its venue: two papers in a cloud journal had no cloud evaluation at all, which suggests weak editing.
- Prefer reproducible work: published code and datasets, open-source tooling. The "Papers with Code" site lists papers with implementations.
- Challenged papers can be retracted; be sure of a claim before making it.
- AI tools can help implement a paper to test its claims, but you must understand the result.

A reviewing method from a student: read three times, mark unclear points as possible errors and missing points as questions.
