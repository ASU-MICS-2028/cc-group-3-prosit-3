title: AI use rules - verify output, never fabricate data
tags: [course, process]
source: Lecture 2; Lecture 6; Lecture 10 (instructor guidance)
---

AI tools are allowed but must not replace thinking. The lecturer's guidance:

- Understand the problem and sketch the design on paper first, so you can trace your own reasoning.
- Good prompts state a role, the context, and the output format and length you want.
- AI can invent plausible numbers. A government policy document drafted with AI was withdrawn because its figures did not match the ministry's real data. Check every figure.
- Do not make up data. Use only data you were told to use, and be ready to defend every number.
- Use AI for labour like debugging or boilerplate; keep human input in the design and analysis.

For SmartCity Accra: cost and sustainability numbers must come from real pricing pages or measurements, with sources.

**Also (Lecture 6):** Using libraries and AI coding assistants is allowed and expected; you will not build everything from scratch. But try things yourself first, understand what the tool does, and remember you, not the AI, are accountable for everything in the slides and project. Team advice: have AI break the requirements into roles for the team, then pick roles by expertise; keep a written daily task list; learn through short videos.

**Also (Lecture 10 (instructor guidance)):** The instructor said AI coding tools make you faster and are normal in industry, but it is dangerous when a tool builds something you cannot explain. In a panel or Q&A you will not be able to check a chatbot. Each member should be able to explain the full system from A to Z and every tool used (define it), including CI/CD and merge rules. Where an implementation is in the spec but not understood, go learn it. The labs exist to teach the pieces.

Practical for the team: during Prosit 3, each person should be able to walk through MQTT, the broker, the database, the model and the scaling setup.
